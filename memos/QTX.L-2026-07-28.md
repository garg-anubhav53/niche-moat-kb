## §4 Moat Assessment

**Moat levers:**
1. **Switching costs (HIGH):** Fleet operators have installed Quartix hardware across entire vehicle fleets. Migration requires physical hardware swap + retraining + data migration. SME operators especially averse to disruption.
2. **Subscription stickiness confirmed:** NRR 98.1% (was 95.7% — improving). While below 100% indicating some net vehicle attrition, the net churn is very low for a £8–10/vehicle/month product sold to SMEs.

4. **Data network effects (weak):** Driver behavior data improves insurance underwriting for PAYG insurance customers — modest lock-in enhancement.
5. **Market position:** UK's established fleet telematics SaaS for SME segment; France and US presence smaller but growing.

**Moat quality score: 1** (switching cost moat exists and is measurable via NRR; CORE-level claim would require >100% NRR or multi-year lock-in contracts; Grade B is appropriate)

## Bear Case (Pre-Red-Team)

1. **NRR<100%** means Quartix is not truly a "sticky" SaaS — it is perpetually replenishing a leaky bucket. If new customer acquisition slows, ARR could reverse.
2. **Single analyst** = illiquidity risk and potential mispricing in either direction. The GBX 360p target may be stale/optimistic.
3. **Competitive**: Samsara (US-listed, $5B cap), Verizon Connect, Radius/Radius Telematics all serve the UK SME market. Quartix's differentiation (simplicity, price) is not patent-protected.
4. **US geography** is a small portion of revenue but could be a drag if not gaining traction.
5. **GM ceiling**: Hardware manufacturing limits GM expansion beyond ~75–78% without moving to asset-light software-only model.
6. **Market re-rate risk**: If interest rates rise further or UK small-cap premium compresses, EV/ARR multiple could stay at 4x indefinitely.
